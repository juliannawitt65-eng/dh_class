About this project

This timeline began as a nine-entry sample made with the Knight Lab TimelineJS Google Sheets template. This revised version corrects facts in the original, replaces hotlinked images with openly licensed ones, and expands it to 27 entries in five groups, so that changes in one medium can be seen next to changes in others.

The timeline is arranged in four eras that historians of sound recording commonly use: acoustic (1877–1925), electrical (1925–45), magnetic (1945–75) and digital (1975 onward). The eras are a framework rather than hard boundaries. Formats from different eras overlapped for decades, and vinyl and cassettes are still being made today.

Methods

Each date was checked against at least two sources, usually the Wikipedia article for the format and a news or institutional account (for example the Library of Congress for the LP and Philips for the cassette). When sources disagree, the slide says so. One example is the LP's launch date, which is given as either June 18 or June 21, 1948. The data is kept in timeline.json. The same data is also provided as a CSV in the Knight Lab template layout, so it can be pasted back into the original Google Sheet.

Ideas for the next version

Make an argument, not only a listChoose one thesis to test across the timeline. Examples: each new format traded fidelity for convenience, or control moved from producers to listeners. Then rewrite each "Why it matters" line to support or complicate it.
Use primary sourcesReplace the Wikipedia links with period advertisements, patents, newspaper reports and catalogues. Good sources are the Library of Congress (Chronicling America, the National Jukebox), the Internet Archive, and Hathi Trust.
Let readers hear and see the formatsTimelineJS can embed audio and video. The National Jukebox, Internet Archive 78s and the UCSB Cylinder Audio Archive have public-domain recordings.
Add who and whereMost entries are about companies in the US, Europe and Japan. Add who used these formats and how: cassette culture in the Global South, pirate radio, mixtapes, hip-hop, and community TV.
Track obsolescence and preservationAdd end dates or "last year manufactured" so each format shows a lifespan. Add a short section on digital preservation: which of these formats can still be played today?
Cite in the slidesAsk your instructor which citation style the course uses (MLA or Chicago) and add short citations to each slide, plus a full bibliography on this page.
